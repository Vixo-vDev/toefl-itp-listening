### Questions 31 – 34: Weekend Plans

- **Conversation:**
  - **Woman:** Do you have any plans this weekend? There's so much to choose from on campus that I'm not sure what I'm gonna do.
  - **Man:** Football game's on Saturday night, and I'm going with a group of friends. Do you want to go with us?
  - **Woman:** Of course I'd like to go to the football game! It's the biggest game of the season, and it sounds like fun to go with a large group of people.
  - **Man:** Good! We'll be meeting at the cafeteria for dinner at 6 o'clock on Saturday night, and then we'll go on to the game together.
  - **Woman:** That takes care of my plans for Saturday night, but now I need to make a decision about Sunday afternoon. The music department is sponsoring a concert then, and I'd really like to hear that concert, but there's also a play being presented by the drama department that I really wanted to see. It's too bad those two events are at the same time.
  - **Man:** You know, if you go to the game on Saturday night and a concert or play on Sunday, that doesn't leave much time for studying.
  - **Woman:** Oh well, maybe I can do that the weekend after this one.

- **Questions:**
  - **Number 31:** What is the woman planning to do Saturday?
  - **Number 32:** Why does the man want to go to the football game?
  - **Number 33:** What is at the same time as the music department's concert?
  - **Number 34:** When does the man plan to study?

### Questions 35 – 38: Space Debris (Garbage in Orbit)

- **Conversation:**
  - **Man:** Have you ever thought about all the tons of garbage that's out in space circling the Earth?
  - **Woman:** Tons of garbage circling the Earth? What do you mean?
  - **Man:** I saw a television program about it last night, and according to the program, there's about 3,000 tons of metal out there in space traveling at speeds around 17,000 miles per hour.
  - **Woman:** Where did all this garbage come from?
  - **Man:** Well, it comes from all those space missions that have gone up since 1957. Every time a rocket ship goes up into space, it leaves a lot behind, and this stuff goes into orbit around the Earth: booster rockets, solar panels, remnants of satellites, and even nuclear reactors.
  - **Woman:** Isn't it dangerous to have all this stuff out there?
  - **Man:** Some space scientists are worried about possible collisions between this orbiting junk and spaceships, particularly manned spacecrafts. However, so far there hasn't been any such accident.
  - **Woman:** Well, I hope that they're going to do something about this, both for the sake of safety and for the sake of the environment.
  - **Man:** Me too. I know that right now the problem is being studied by numerous scientists. Hopefully, they'll be able to find solutions before the problem gets too much worse.

- **Questions:**
  - **Number 35:** What are the man and woman discussing?
  - **Number 36:** Where did the woman learn about this problem?
  - **Number 37:** Approximately how much metal is in orbit in space?
  - **Number 38:** What does the woman hope will happen?

### Questions 43 – 46: Orientation Briefing on Hawaii Weather

- **Talk:**
  - **Speaker:** For those of you taking part in the trip to Hawaii next week, I'd like to give you a little information about the weather that you can expect there. You can expect the average daily temperature there to be about 80 degrees Fahrenheit or 26 degrees Celsius. This is the average daily temperature in the springtime when we will be there. It is interesting to note that it only gets a few degrees warmer in the summer and a few degrees cooler in the winter. One important factor that keeps the temperature so constant and moderate in Hawaii is the trade winds. These are winds that blow in on the northeast or windward side of the islands on an almost daily basis. The trade winds blow through the islands an average of slightly more than 300 days per year, and they are the strongest during the heat of the afternoon and turn into a cooling breeze in the evening. The trade winds also keep the humidity down, which makes the weather even more pleasant. I hope this information will help you to understand the weather conditions that you're going to encounter next week on your trip. It should also help you decide what types of clothes you should be packing for your trip.

- **Questions:**
  - **Number 43:** In what season of the year will the trip take place?
  - **Number 44:** What is the weather like in Hawaii?
  - **Number 45:** What is true about the trade winds?
  - **Number 46:** What will the people listening to the talk probably be doing soon?

### Questions 47 – 50: Literature Lecture on Walt Whitman

- **Talk:**
  - **Speaker (Instructor):** The topic of today's lecture is Walt Whitman, an American poet and author of the renowned collection of poems *Leaves of Grass*. This volume of poems is a celebration of America, full of pride in the United States and reverence for the goals of American democracy. Whitman began writing *Leaves of Grass* in the middle of the nineteenth century, and the first edition appeared in 1855 with only twelve poems. Several other editions of *Leaves of Grass* appeared throughout Whitman's lifetime with additional poems. *Leaves of Grass* grew and matured right along with Whitman. The longest and best-known poem in *Leaves of Grass* is "Song of Myself", which appeared in the first edition. The poem "When Lilacs Last in the Dooryard Bloomed" was added to a later edition. This poem was written at the time of Abraham Lincoln's death in 1865 and contained Whitman's reflections on that event. Lincoln's death occurred in April, in the spring, in a season of new life. This poem reflects that spring can be at the same time a period of death and a period of rebirth.

- **Questions:**
  - **Number 47:** This lecture would probably be a part of which course?
  - **Number 48:** What is the most common theme in *Leaves of Grass*?
  - **Number 49:** What best describes *Leaves of Grass*?
  - **Number 50:** Which is the longest and best-known poem by Whitman?
